About the Practice

Who I am and what I do.

Who is Kristina Pulfer?

I'm a landscape designer and plant consultant based in Salt Lake City, Utah, with a decade of experience in ecological and native planting design. I trained at Utah State University, and previously served as Senior Garden Designer for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, leading planting design on one of Utah's most high-profile historic landscapes. I specialize in native and water-resilient planting, naturalistic garden design, and plant consulting for larger architecture and landscape design firms.

Where are you based?

I'm based in Salt Lake City, working close to home in the city's older neighborhoods — the Avenues, Yalecrest, and the historic grounds of Temple Square. I serve clients throughout the Salt Lake Valley, with plant consulting engagements further afield.

What areas do you serve?

I serve clients throughout the Salt Lake Valley, with a particular concentration on Salt Lake City's historic neighborhoods and an ongoing relationship at Temple Square. I also take on plant consulting for firms outside Utah, and have designed native gardens as far away as Australia.

What types of projects do you take on?

Primarily residential gardens — new planting plans, garden renovations, and ongoing consulting for established gardens. I also take on select civic and institutional planting work, and plant consulting for larger firms already leading a project. My work ranges from a single backyard bed to civic-scale grounds.

What's the most notable project you've worked on?

An ongoing planting relationship with the historic grounds of Temple Square in Salt Lake City — one of Utah's most high-profile landscapes, where I've led native and water-resilient planting design at civic scale.

The Process

From first conversation to years of established growth.

What is the process for starting a project with a landscape designer?

It begins with a conversation. I want to understand your site, how you use the space, and what drew you to the garden or the land in the first place. From there, I move through a planting concept, a detailed plant palette and layout, installation coordination, and ongoing maintenance guidance. I stay present at every stage.

How long does a residential garden take from design to established?

A typical residential garden takes one to three growing seasons from first meeting to full establishment. Design and planting usually happen within a single season, but naturalistic plantings need two to three years to knit together and reach their intended form.

At what stage should I bring in a landscape designer — before or after buying a home?

Before, whenever possible. The site is the most important design input — its sun, slope, existing plant life, and soil all determine what a garden can become. I've helped clients evaluate a property's outdoor potential before purchase, and in several cases identified opportunities that changed the decision.

Do you handle installation and contractor coordination?

I don't handle permitting or hardscape construction — that stays with a licensed landscape architect, architect, or contractor. What I do handle is planting coordination: reviewing plant material on site, walking the layout with the install crew, and providing regular site visits during installation.

Do you offer garden maintenance services?

Yes. Maintenance guidance is integrated into my design practice. I set up a maintenance plan for a garden's first few seasons, and can stay on in an ongoing advisory role for homeowners or maintenance crews who want a naturalistic garden cared for correctly.

Methods & Materials

How and why I plant the way I do.

What makes your approach different?

Two things, primarily. First, I design from the site inward — sun, slope, soil, and existing plant life shape the palette before anything else does. Second, I plant with water resilience and native species, rather than the thirsty, high-input plants that dominate conventional landscaping.

What is water-resilient planting and why do you use it?

Water-resilient planting means choosing native and climate-adapted species already built for this valley's water, soil, and light — so a garden needs less irrigation as it establishes, not more. It's the operating principle behind the most resilient plant communities in this region: the ones that were never irrigated at all.

What is hydrozoning?

Hydrozoning means grouping plants by water need, so a bed is watered to what it actually requires rather than to satisfy its thirstiest neighbor. It's fundamentally different from a single fixed irrigation schedule, which either overwaters drought-tolerant plants or underwaters thirsty ones.

Do you design fully xeriscape, no-water gardens?

I share xeriscape's commitment to reduced water use, and incorporate its principles — hydrozoning, mulch, native selection — into every project. However, I don't limit myself to gravel-only planting, as a well-chosen palette of climate-adapted perennials can achieve the same water savings with far more seasonal beauty and habitat value.

Can you work on historic gardens and civic sites?

Historic and civic work is a core part of my practice.

Fees & Budget

Honest answers about cost.

How much does it cost to hire a landscape designer?

I work either on a retainer or at an hourly rate of $150, depending on the project. A single-visit consultation is billed hourly; a full planting plan is scoped and quoted once I understand the site and how much of the process you'd like me involved in.

How are your fees typically structured?

Fees are typically structured as an hourly rate for smaller residential consults, or a retainer for larger or ongoing engagements — plant consulting for a firm, multi-season maintenance oversight. I don't charge for an initial conversation.

Can you work within a tighter budget?

Yes. The most powerful tool for delivering a beautiful, resilient garden at an accessible cost is a well-designed plant palette. A smaller, disciplined selection of self-seeding, spreading perennials fills in and multiplies rather than needing replacement — less input, less cost, over the life of the garden.

Where We Work

Our markets.

What were some of your roles at Temple Square?
