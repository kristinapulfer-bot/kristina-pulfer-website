Process

Every project starts differently — a bare foundation planting, an established garden that's lost its way, a civic border that needs to carry a full season of bloom. But the path from a first conversation to a garden that's established and thriving tends to follow the same throughline.

It begins with a conversation, usually by phone or email, to understand the site, the scope of the work, and what you're hoping the garden will become.

If it feels like the right fit, I'll visit the site in person. This is time spent walking the ground, reading its sun and slope, and looking closely at what's already growing there. First impressions of a site's light, soil, and existing plant life shape everything that follows.

From there, I develop a planting concept — a palette and a layout suited to the site's conditions and to how you'll use the space. This is shared and refined with you before any planting begins, so the final plan reflects both the site's demands and your own hopes for the garden.

For sites that need grading, drainage, or built structure, I work alongside a licensed landscape architect, architect, or contractor, and keep my own focus on what I do best: the planting.

Once a plan is set, I coordinate installation — reviewing plant material on site, walking the layout with the install crew, and staying present through planting.

A naturalistic garden isn't finished the day it's planted. I stay engaged through the first one to three growing seasons, with a maintenance plan and regular visits to help the garden establish and knit together as intended — and I'm glad to continue in an ongoing advisory role for homeowners or maintenance crews who want a garden cared for correctly.
